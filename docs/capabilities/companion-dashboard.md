## What I can do

On Family, each property's page, System and each workspace's page, I show the
section tabs below the header and above the summary metrics.

My Right Now suggestion appears as a compact metric. Click it to open a side
drawer with the reasoning, evidence and existing Talk it through and Places &
projects options. It still appears only when there is a timely suggestion or
an ongoing or imminent event, outside working hours. Close the drawer with
Escape, the close button or the backdrop.

The top strip shows your latest WHOOP sleep duration and, when available, the
percentage of the sleep your body asked for. It is separate from Recovery, so
you can see both how long you slept and how ready your body is. Tap it to open
Health & Performance. Missing or disconnected sleep data stays clearly marked;
I do not estimate it.

I populate the cards from connected sources: Google Calendar events for the next
seven days; dated WHOOP recovery, sleep and strain;
the linked Family Chores player's chores for today — everything still open and due today or earlier, overdue ones included, plus what was finished today, with a View all link to Family Chores; saved family and work
memories; assigned Jira issues; unread Gmail headers; Slack mentions; and
pending action approvals. A missing connection, missing health consent, empty
result and failed read are distinct states. I do not invent missing metrics.

The Family card is one row per person I remember, most pressing first: someone
under the weather (with a Feeling better? button), a birthday in the next two
weeks, something on your calendar that names them, someone I haven't been told
about in three weeks (a nudge to update me and check on them), or someone I
barely know (a nudge to tell me more). The order is worked out by plain rules,
not by me guessing. It never goes quiet: with nobody remembered it asks you to
tell me about your family, and Add someone is always there. I can't see texts or
calls yet, so "staying in touch" means how long since you last told me about
someone, not when you last spoke.

Calendar items on the Family card show the event details without a Talk it
through button.

Each person on the card carries a small tag for how they're related to you:
Spouse, Son, Daughter, Cousin, Pet, Child. I take it from how the memory is
filed ("spouse"), from a pet, or from what you told me ("Wynter is my
daughter") — never from someone else's mention ("lives near her son"). When I
don't know how someone is related to you, their row says so and the button opens
a chat where I ask, so I can fill it in. What you tell me there goes onto that
person's own memory, not a new entry beside them, and a detail I once filed
under its own name ("Aunt Missy relation", "Skylar birthday") shows on the
person's row rather than as a person of its own — I fold those back in myself
the next time we talk. A relation word that opens their name ("Aunt Missy") says
how they're related. Household facts filed with the family ("children home
time", "family dinner") aren't people and don't appear on the card.

You can set that tag yourself: open the Family page, tap the person's card,
change Relationship to you (up to 40 characters), and Save. The ⋯ menu in the
Family Members → Memories drawer also has Edit.
I remember your label and use it ahead of the relationship in their older
memory, keeping everything else I know about them. This control is available
for remembered people and pets. For a child listed only from a family profile,
saving the editor creates a memory linked by their name; the family profile
continues to supply their birthday and grade.

Children on your family profiles appear on the card on their own, with their
birthday, and a child you have also told me about is one row, not two. On the
Family Members → Memories opens a side drawer where each person I remember has
a ⋯ menu (Health watch, Link contact,
Edit, Move to Community, Merge, Delete). Link contact lets you search your
Google Contacts and pick the one that is them, and I read their birthday, photo
and phone number from there. Only the contact's ID and name are kept, so a
change in Google shows up on its own, and Unlink removes it. If Google Contacts
isn't connected, the same button offers to connect it. A contact can be linked
to only one person.

When the same person or pet has been remembered twice, or I filed something that
isn't a person ("Name", "Parents"), the Family page can tidy it. Merge on a
person asks who they should be folded into: what I know about them is added to
that person (nothing repeated, nothing lost), their Google Contact link moves
across if the target has none, and the duplicate is forgotten. Delete forgets an
entry after you confirm. Children from your family profiles can't be merged or
deleted here.

When I've got something wrong — "dad's sister" when she's only your mom's
sister — Edit lets you rewrite what I remember about that person in your own
words; your wording replaces mine, and I only change it again if you clearly
tell me something different in conversation.
Edit opens one dialog with their name, relationship, remembered notes and any
separately saved details, such as their birthday. Name changes appear on both
the Family page and card; I show a person's name rather than the memory key
("Spouse"). The dialog also lets you link, change or unlink their Google
Contact, choose a private picture from your device, and open their health
watch. A chosen picture takes priority over the Google Contact photo, appears
on your other devices, and can be removed to show the contact photo again.
On Family → Pets, tap a pet's row to open this editor. You can also remove a
pet there after confirming; its family memory and chosen picture are removed.
Contact changes save immediately; Save saves the memory fields and picture.
Contact phone numbers and birthdays are edited in Google Contacts.
Someone who isn't family at all — the neighbor's dog — can go to the
Community page with Move to Community: pick their household (or give an
address for a new one), what I knew is added to that household's notes, and
they leave your family list.

The News card and page show headlines I have already read for you in the
background, from pages you paste in News → Manage pages — no feed URL needed,
and no waiting on a website when you open the dashboard. How often I read each
page is mine to work out, not yours to set; news watching has its own
description. A page I could not read last time says so on the card. The
dashboard reads data; it never connects accounts or approves actions by itself.

The Dashboard opens on "Your day at a glance": a header with your greeting.
I don't call a day calm just because some sources are missing.
Around me in the header sit your areas of
life — Health (GO, REST or WARNING and recovery), Family (anyone unwell, a
birthday, or how many people I know), Work (open Jira issues), Property (open
issues or concerns), Dreams (nights I've dreamt in the last 30 days) and
Community (an alert, calls nearby, or what's coming up) — each coloured by how
it stands and each opening its page. My Right Now metric appears in the stat
strip when timely and when the Work banner is not showing.

Latest changes shows the most recent dated records from WHOOP,
Jira and my dream journal. Recovery compares two distinct scored dates and names
both; it doesn't assume the previous reading was yesterday. Jira shows the latest
updated open issue and its actual status. Dreams shows the most recently finished
successful or partial dream. These are dated source records, not a complete
activity history or a comparison with your last visit. Each opens its area page.

Under the header, a strip of
tiles: Recovery, today's Gym session from your training plan (or Rest day, or
No plan), your Next event, Open tasks (Jira),
and the weather at your home point of interest from the National Weather
Service. Recovery shows its reading date, Calendar and Jira their check times,
and weather its forecast check time. Next event has a countdown that moves as
the clock advances. Then the cards: Your day (the next three days that have
events in a compact scrollable preview, expandable all-day entries, a Now marker and muted ended calendar events;
an ended event does not mean you attended), Work (your Jira issues with a tab
per Jira site and their status), Projects (non-work projects with a tab per area
and a bar for steps done, or No steps set). Your day, Work and Projects match
heights when side by side; the schedule preview keeps its scrollable height limit.
The Work card's header has a single View all link to the full Work page.
Health & Performance, Family, Property and Dreams share a 400-pixel card height;
longer content scrolls inside each card while its header stays visible.
Below them are
Health & Performance (the recovery ring, the verdict and its reason, and
resting heart rate, HRV, sleep and weight with two weeks of bars under each),
Family, Property (the property's header picture, open issues and concerns),
and Dreams (how many nights I dreamt this month, my latest dream with its
painting, and Ask her about it). Health then Family still lead the area cards,
following your own hierarchy: take care of yourself, so you can take care of
your family, so your family can take care of the community. Every tile and card
whose app isn't connected says so and carries the button that fixes it.
Topic shortcuts open our chat
with an editable question; you choose when to send it. What is waiting for
your approval lives on the bell in the top bar, visible from any screen —
there is no separate Notifications card any more. The bell opens Notifications:
every notification I have pushed to your phone or browser that you haven't
acknowledged yet (each stays until you tap Acknowledge here or on the phone
notification's own Acknowledge button, so tapping a notification and losing it
no longer happens; the last week of acknowledged ones is listed underneath),
every proposal waiting for your approval, which you can approve or decline right
there, and the questions my dreaming left me holding, which I'll ask when you
tap Answer in chat. Its badge counts all three. What I may do and what I have done
is one tap further, under Actions & permissions. Swiping a phone notification
away does not acknowledge it; it is still waiting in the bell.

While your calendar says you're working, a banner sits across the top of the
Dashboard: where you're working ("Home"), until when, and the one thing the day
wants next — in the order a day goes: where you're working, the Jira ticket you
have In Progress — or, with none, the one Ready to Start (tap it to open it in
Jira), then the next meeting and
how long you have before it. While you're in a meeting, that meeting comes
second and the ticket follows it. If nothing is In Progress or Ready to Start there is no ticket
block, and if nothing else is left before you finish it says so. It is
driven by the Working location you set in Google Calendar, not by the clock: no
location covering the moment, or an Out of office event over it, and there is no
banner, so a day off needs nothing from you. It appears and disappears on its
own at the start and end of that block. Only meetings inside the working block
count as "next meeting"; focus time and all-day markers don't. And only meetings
on the same calendar as that Working location — your work calendar — count: a
family or shared calendar's events stay on the Calendar card, not in the banner.

"In progress" is Jira's own started grouping, whatever your workflow calls it
("Operational process" counts), minus the hand-offs: a ticket in Code Review,
QA, Pending Prod Testing or the like is waiting on someone else, so it never
takes that spot. If several are in progress I show the one
you touched last and say how many more. Beside the banner, when Jira is
connected, a small chip says how many open issues are assigned to you and how
many are in progress — or, if any have a due date, how many are overdue or due
today. Tap it to open Work. When Jira has more than I read, the count reads
"25+" rather than claiming a total. If Jira isn't connected or can't be read,
neither the ticket nor the chip is there; the Work card says why.

The Community card shows what is happening around your points of interest —
nearby 911 calls and National Weather Service alerts, including the quiet ones
that never became a banner — and the next local event you've saved. The
Community page is where you keep your points of interest, neighbors and local
events (see Community). Family and
Work show up to three saved memories about people, pets, or work, explicitly
labeled as memories. Memories, photos, and connected apps have direct shortcuts
too. My controls provide hover, press, and keyboard-focus feedback. I respect
your device's reduced-motion preference by disabling interaction movement.

The compact Daily briefing drawer in Chat keeps the earlier card set, one
headline per card. There, the cards are always in the same order: Health & Performance, Family,
Community, then the rest. Health leads with the verdict — GO, REST or WARNING
and the reasons behind it — then the last seven days of recovery and of sleep
as bars, so a bad day reads as a bad day or a trend — and keeps the individual
numbers behind Show the numbers. Calendar leads with what is on now or what is next and how long until
it starts (no drive time; I only know the place's name). On a phone each card
folds to its title and the one thing worth knowing — GO for Health, the next
event for Calendar, open mail, open issues — tap the title to open or fold it
and the headline to go to the page; I remember which you folded on that
browser. On a larger screen every card stays open. I don't re-sort them — you asked me not to. What I do read is the counts and
timings behind each card, to decide whether anything deserves the alert
banner across the top of the screen; most days nothing does. My general
dashboard alerts are heads-ups. Only verified nearby calls or weather warnings
appear as emergencies near home. A calendar entry never tells me whether a
flight departed or someone missed it, so I won't raise a flight-status alert
from it.

The Calendar page lays out the next seven days: how many events, how many
today and how many of those are done, what's next, and which calendars they
come from. A strip of the seven days picks the day shown below it — each event
with its times, a coloured bar for what it's about (work, family, health,
sports, travel or personal, judged from the words in the title), a countdown
on the next one, and ⋯ to ask me about it. Beside it are a month (dots mark
only the days I've read), the next two days, and any trips or time off. At
the bottom: Focus Areas (tap one to show only those events), Quick Add (time
block, reminder, workout, family event, travel — each starts a message to me,
and I ask before anything goes on your calendar), and your calendars with a
switch that hides one from this page.

The Family page opens with my take on the family's week — how much is on the
calendar and what it is (soccer, softball, school), anyone reported unwell, a
birthday coming up, and any family events that overlap — written from what the
page shows, not guessed. Then: people remembered, today's chores, the next
family event and family health; today's chores and the family's day; your
closest people as cards (spouse, children, pets) with what's on for them today,
and everyone else as smaller tiles; the family's upcoming events; and Kids
(age, grade, and the sports their events mention), Pets, and Birthdays (from
what I remember and from birthdays on your calendar). Tapping a member opens
their editor. The small Memories link at the bottom of Family Members opens a
side drawer with the full memory list, contact links, and health watches.

There is no refresh button, because there is nothing to refresh by hand: the
dashboard updates itself when the server tells it something moved.

I reuse recently read information so reopening Dashboard or the daily briefing can
load faster. Routine background checks keep the cards steady while they load.
Changes announced by the server clear the previous view and request new data.

## Where to find it

After signing in to Companion, I open Dashboard. On desktop, the left sidebar
contains Dashboard, topic navigation and Talk to Athena. On mobile, the bottom bar
has Dashboards, Chat, the central Athena button, Alerts (notifications), and More.
Dashboards opens a menu of every page the desktop sidebar lists — Dashboard,
Health, Family, Community, Property, Work and System — with the page you're on
highlighted. Calendar, Mail, Projects, News and Dreams are tabs of System (with
Overview and Finance), and the cards on Dashboard open them directly. Your
browser address follows the selected page and tab, so a link to System → Mail
or Property → Security opens that view directly, and Back returns to the previous
view. A property link uses that property's ID in the address once it loads.
Your name at the bottom of the sidebar — or More on mobile — opens one menu holding
every setting and panel: memories, photos, brain, phone and car, local server,
actions, initiative, connected apps, voice, and sign out. In Chat, Daily
briefing opens a compact drawer with an Open full dashboard button.
When Google provides a profile picture at sign-in, I show it beside your name
in the sidebar; if it cannot be loaded, I show your initial instead.

## When it doesn't work

When a card can't read a source, the fix is on the card, right under the line
that says so: "Google Calendar · Reconnect to refresh" carries a Reconnect
button that goes straight to that provider's sign-in and back (WHOOP recovery,
sleep and strain share one button), missing health consent carries Give
consent, and a failed read carries Retry. An app you never linked says "Not
connected" with a Connect button that goes straight to that provider, and
every card stays on the dashboard even when none of its apps are linked yet.
When the app shares health data (WHOOP) and you haven't agreed to that
yet, Connect and Give consent open Connected apps straight on that app's
consent prompt; "I agree — connect" then goes on to its sign-in. Nothing is
agreed for you — the prompt still waits for your tap. Section pages carry the same buttons in
the panel.

Empty cards say what would fill them, with the button that does: Add a news
page when you have none (and Fix this page when one stopped answering), Scan
more on an empty Mail card, Tell her about them when I know no family, Tell
her what you're working towards when there are no saved goals, Add a point of
interest on a Community card with no places, and Retry when memories,
nearby activity, approvals or news failed to load. A Tell her button only
starts our chat — nothing is saved until you say it.

If a Reconnect can't start from the card, I open Connected apps instead, which
says why. A topic shortcut only fills the chat input; press
Send when you want my answer. Existing sign-in and access
requirements still apply.

## Limits

News requires the news-watch database migration and its scheduled job; without
that job nothing is read and the card stays empty. Work OAuth apps
must be configured by the server owner and then authorized by the user. Provider
results are bounded previews, not complete exports. Dated health metrics may be
from earlier days; a missing score is displayed as unknown, never zero.

The header's wave art, landscape and my breathing halo are decorative. Weather
comes from the National Weather Service at your home point of interest (no home
place, no weather). The event-preparation Current Focus card is not available;
the owner deferred it. Jira's open
issue preview cannot establish completed work; I never label disappearing issues
as completed. I don't save a personal visit history in the browser. The Dashboard no
longer has a News or Community card — News is under System → News, and
Community's details are on its page — and the Gym tile only knows the training
plan you set on Health. I never present sample personal
data as yours. Opening a card does not send a message or approve an action, and
I can't reorder the cards. The Calendar page shows only the
seven days I read — the month beyond them is blank, not empty — and its focus
areas are a guess from each title. Its calendar switches hide events on that
page in this browser only; they don't change Google Calendar or what I read. On the Family page you can tick
your own chores done (with a few seconds to Undo), but I can't add or edit
chores — they come from Family Chores — and the sports tags are words found in
calendar titles that mention the child. Live updates only reach a dashboard whose
connection is healthy; when the socket is down it falls back to re-reading
quietly in the background, which is slower but never wrong.

Connected-provider reads may reuse results for up to 30 seconds, and my browser
can reuse dashboard reads for another 15 seconds. External changes appear on
the next refresh; background polling runs every five minutes. I reuse an alert
judgement only when its input signals match, for at most ten minutes. These
caches do not reuse our chat replies or approve anything. Browser copies stay
in memory and are cleared on sign-in, sign-out, access errors, and updates.
