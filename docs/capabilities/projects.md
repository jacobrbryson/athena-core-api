## What I can do

I hold your projects — the goal, the status, the steps, rough effort and cost
(what it'd cost you yourself and hired out), the season and weather it needs,
and a hard deadline if there is one. A project can belong to more than one part
of your life, with one as its home: coaching can be Community and Family at
once.

Projects connect. One can be **part of** a bigger program — painting the office
is part of the interior painting program — and one can **need another first**:
painting the garage waits on finding the smell. While something it needs is
still open, I show it as blocked, and it unblocks itself when that's done.

I also keep **concerns** — the problems your projects exist to solve, like a
musty crawlspace — and your **charter** for an area: your vision, your rules,
and how much each kind of value counts to you. I order your projects from those
in plain arithmetic: priority, how serious the open concerns behind them are,
your charter's weights, and how close a deadline is. I can tell you why
something is near the top.

Each project has a journal: photos marked concept, before, progress, after or
memory, plus notes, decisions and measurements. Photos of your own projects are
stored privately and only come back to you. **Photos of children never leave
your device** — on a project that involves children, or any photo you mark as
having children in it, only the caption is saved and the picture stays on the
phone or computer that took it.

You can also tell me in chat — "I finished the gutters", "add buying batts to
the attic project", "start a project to fix the fence", "the posts are waiting
on the lumber delivery, check Thursday", "the gate can't go in until the posts
are set", "let's do the fence line Saturday at 9", "Saturday went fine, about an
hour", "move the version to 1.3, budget $3,400" — and I'll propose the change
for you to approve, naming the project as it appears in your list. Closing out
a project with "done when" checks, and putting a session on your calendar,
always ask you first, every time.

A project can also read data I already have, live — **Connected data** in the
project: your **weight** from the smart scale (compared with the straight line
from your start to your target across the project's dates, so I can say "on
track", "ahead" or "behind" and by how much), your **WHOOP workouts** for the
sports you pick (sessions per week, the last one), and **calendar events**
whose titles contain words you choose (the next 60 days). The plan line also
appears as a dashed line on the Weight tab of the Health page's Progress card.

For a build — a fence, a deck, an awning — a project also holds the plan
itself. **Move to front** puts it at the front of the line, above everything
my arithmetic would rank first; the newest one you moved goes first, and
**Unpin** hands it back to the usual order. It can carry your **reference
code** and **version** (HOME-SIDEYARD-2026-001 v1.2), and **New version…**
bumps the version and writes what changed into its journal as a decision. A
**working budget** and an **approval ceiling** sit beside the cost range.
**Specs & verification** lists each measurement or decision — fence run 32 ft,
gate width 4 ft — marked **Confirmed**, **To verify** or **Estimate**, so it's
plain what's settled and what still needs checking. A **Materials takeoff**
lists quantities with an optional cost range, totals the priced lines and says
whether the high end is inside the working budget or over the ceiling.

**What you can do next.** Steps can **need other steps first** ("can't pour
until the forms are built"). Order on the list is only display order — only a
"needs first" makes a step wait. A step is **ready** when it isn't done,
nothing it needs is still open, it isn't blocked, and the project itself isn't
on hold or waiting on another project. Each project shows its **Next action**:
the most useful ready step and why it's ready ("everything it needs is done",
"fits the two hours you have", "indoors, and it's wet out"). When nothing is
ready I say why and what would change that, instead of the project going
quiet.

**Blockers get looked at again.** When a step is blocked I keep what's in the
way, **who or what it's waiting on**, **when to check back**, and **what would
clear it**. When the check-back day comes, it shows as a follow-up — on the
project, in Right Now and when we talk — once per check-back date, never
repeated. A blocker with no check-back date is flagged so you can pick one. A
check-back date coming round never unblocks anything by itself.

**Time to do it.** You can set aside **work sessions** — for a project or one
step, as many as it takes. I suggest a realistic block from the step's effort
and your free time, and before a session I show what's planned, the materials,
what still needs verifying, anything still in the way and the weather it
needs. Afterwards I ask how it went; a session being over never ticks off its
step — only you say the step is done. A session goes on your Google Calendar
only when you ask (**Add to calendar**, or approving my card), and one session
is only ever one event; rescheduling keeps the same session, and **Update
calendar** moves its event.

**What finished looks like.** A project can say **Done when** — one check per
line ("Gate opens and latches correctly") — and a step can have its own
acceptance criteria. Every step done isn't the same as finished: I show
whether there's work remaining, every step is done but the outcome isn't
confirmed yet, it's ready for you to close out, or it's finished and you
confirmed it. Closing out a project with "done when" checks means ticking
each one; I never certify structural or safety work myself. Projects without
checks close the way they always did.

**Finishing over starting.** I lean toward moving your active projects forward
before suggesting new ones — about three at once by default (**Focus** on the
Projects tab). Urgent repairs, safety work, close deadlines and anything you
moved to the front still come first. It's a preference for what I suggest,
never a limit: I don't pause, drop or reorder anything without you.

Right Now uses all of this: it suggests the ready step that fits your free time
(saying when it's a first sitting of something bigger), a follow-up whose day
has come, or a project with no steps that fits — never blocked work or a step
whose prerequisites are open — and says when a project is stuck and what would
unstick it. Work projects stay on your Work page and off the Dashboard.

## Where to find it

- **System → Projects** (a tab of System; it left the left menu 2026-10-16): every project, filterable by area, with a
  search box and **New project**. Tap one to open it.
- **Property** in the left menu: the house and land — open projects, programs with their
  parts, your concerns, and your charter.
- Health, Family and Community each show the projects that touch them, and
  Work has its own Work projects panel.
- The Dashboard has a **Projects** card and a **Property** card.

Tapping a project anywhere — Property's **Up next**, the Projects tab, an area
page — opens **its own page**: Next action, Done when, the Project plan (tick
steps, ⋯ for what one needs first or what it's blocked on), Work sessions,
Budget & materials, Photos, what's blocked or still to verify, and Related
projects, with the target date, budget, completion, status and season across
the top. From Property it stays inside Property; **‹ … projects** goes back.
Steps can be grouped into **phases** ("Design and Approvals", "Concrete and
Foundations"): set a step's phase under its ⋯ (or when adding it), or tell me
"put these three under Concrete". The plan then shows each phase with how many
of its steps are done and its dates, the one with the next action opened. A
phase is only a label — it never makes a step wait; "needs first" does that.
**Edit project** opens the full editor.

Inside the editor you can change anything: Start, Mark done, Hold (and say what
it's waiting on), Drop or Reopen in one tap, plus every field, its steps (add,
tick off, reorder, remove), its connections to other projects, and the concerns
it answers, its specs and materials (tap a status chip to move it from To
verify to Confirmed), plus **Photos & notes** — Add a photo (pick its phase and a caption)
or add a note or decision. Delete is at the bottom and asks first — Drop keeps
the history.

## When it doesn't work

- **"Projects are unavailable right now."** The server can't read the projects
  tables — usually the projects database update hasn't been installed yet.
  Nothing is lost; try again once it has.
- **A link was refused.** I won't let two projects wait on each other, or make
  something part of its own part. Remove the other link first.
- **"Photos of children stay on your device."** That project involves
  children, so I can't store the picture — add it again and it will be kept on
  this device with just its caption saved.
- **A photo says "Kept on the device that took it".** It's a device-only photo
  taken on another phone or browser, or that device's storage was cleared.
- **Connected data says "That source isn't connected" or "Health data
  sharing hasn't been agreed to."** Link the scale, WHOOP or Google Calendar in
  Connected apps (and agree to health data sharing for the scale), then reopen
  the project.
- **No plan line.** The project needs a target and start/end dates — the
  Target and When sections.
- **Something shows as blocked that you think is ready.** Open it and look at
  "Needs first" — one of those is still open. Finish or drop it, or remove the
  link. For a step, its **Needs** line names the step it's waiting on.
- **"That would make … wait on each other."** Two steps can't each need the
  other; remove one of the "needs first" links.
- **"That work is already scheduled at that time."** There's already a session
  for that step then — move that one instead.
- **"Confirm the outcome to close it out."** The project has "done when"
  checks — use **Close out…** and tick them, or edit the checks.
- **Add to calendar fails.** Google Calendar isn't linked or needs
  reconnecting (Connected apps). The session stays planned on the project
  either way.

## Limits

- I don't change your projects myself. In chat I propose the change and you
  approve it; everything else is you, in your app.
- I don't order materials, call contractors or price jobs yet — costs are
  numbers you or your import gave me.
- In chat I can change a project's version, budget, ceiling, "done when", its
  place at the front, and add or verify a spec or material, on an approval
  card. Editing a material's cost range, or reordering steps, is in the app.
- I don't move or delete a calendar event when you reschedule or delete a
  session — the session moves, and **Update calendar** (or my card) moves the
  event. I don't read your free time to suggest a session yet unless you tell
  me when you're free; I suggest from the step's effort.
- Follow-ups show on the project, in Right Now and in chat. I don't send a
  push notification for them.
- I don't draw site plans or layouts; a drawing you make can go in the
  project's photos.
- Connected data covers weight, WHOOP workouts and calendar events so far —
  not Jira, GitHub, email or contacts yet. Only weight has a target line; I
  don't track nutrition.
- Calendar matching looks ahead 60 days only, by words in the title.
- I don't look at your photos or describe them; a photo is what you captioned it.
- Device-only photos don't sync between your devices.
- Sharing a project with your household isn't available yet; every project is
  private to you.
